## About Me

I didn't start out in cybersecurity. I spent about a decade in the U.S. Navy as a helicopter search and rescue crewman. That work ran on checklists, operational security, and staying calm when things went wrong. After the Navy I spent five years in wealth management, handling PII and $300M+ in client assets under SEC and FINRA rules.

<!-- PERSONALIZE: replace this paragraph with your own "why cyber" story -->
My interest in security started in that finance job. I was the person clients called when they were locked out of their portal or having trouble with multi-factor authentication. Checking that a caller really was the account owner, and spotting login activity that didn't look right, showed me how much damage one compromised account could do. I wanted to be the person finding and fixing those weaknesses, not just escalating them. That's why I enrolled at SANS.edu, and it's how I ended up as a Vulnerability Engineer. Now I take vulnerabilities from discovery through validation to remediation.

## Capture the Flag (CTF) Experience

### Reflection

<!-- PERSONALIZE: edit to reflect your own experience -->
The National Cyber League (NCL) was my first big CTF season, and it showed me how much I still had to learn. Certification study gave me the concepts. The CTF made me use them on a clock, against problems that didn't come with instructions. The Team Game was the most useful part. Dividing categories by strength, checking each other's answers before submitting, and teaching each other techniques mattered as much as technical skill. That's how we finished with 100% completion and high accuracy. It also matched my day job. Recon, enumeration, and figuring out what's actually exploitable are what vulnerability engineering is built on.
